## Frage

Übersetze Kapitel 4: Die Kritik der digitalen Urteilskraft von de nach en

## Antwort

The machine's judgment: swift as light, precise as mathematics, incorruptible as physics.
It sorts millions of images, recognizes faces in seconds, translates languages in real-time, diagnoses illnesses with certainty.
Yet Kant poses the question: "Is this judgment or merely calculation?"
The machine has no experience, only data. It has no intuition, only patterns. It has no wisdom, only statistics.
Its judgment is always perfect and always incomplete. It sees everything and understands nothing. It evaluates everything and does not know what value is.
Man, on the other hand, judges with his whole being: with intellect and feeling, with experience and hope, with reason and intuition.
His judgment is often flawed, but it is his own. It is slow, but it is considered. It is imperfect, but it is human.
In the encounter of machine precision and human imprecision lies the new power of judgment:
Not the replacement of one by the other, but the complementarity of calculation and reflection.
The machine extends the reach of judgment, man preserves the depth of judgment.
Thus arises the critical power of judgment of the digital age: neither blindly trusting nor blindly rejecting, but examining, distinguishing, responsible.
